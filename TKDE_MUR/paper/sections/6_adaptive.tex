\section{Response-Aware Selection}
\label{sec:adaptive}

The theory suggests a practical design: use inexpensive context features to
narrow the search, then spend predictor evaluations where the response can
change the decision.

\subsection{Cached screening}

Let $h_Q$, $h_A$ and $h_j$ denote representations of the query, the selected
set and candidate $j$. A cached scorer estimates
\begin{equation}
 \widehat m_C(j\mid A)=g_C(h_Q,h_A,h_j)
\end{equation}
for every remaining candidate and retains the top-$q$ set
$\mathcal Q_A$. This stage requires no new predictor evaluation.

\subsection{Response-aware reranking}

For each shortlisted candidate, the fixed predictor is evaluated before and
after the addition. The response is
\begin{equation}
 d_{A,j}=f_{A\cup\{j\}}(x)-f_A(x),
\end{equation}
and a compact summary $r_{A,j}=\psi(f_A,f_{A\cup\{j\}})$ records the
prediction level and simple statistics of the change. A response-aware scorer
then estimates
\begin{equation}
 \widehat m_R(j\mid A)
 =g_R(h_Q,h_A,h_j,r_{A,j}),
 \qquad j\in\mathcal Q_A .
\end{equation}
The candidate with the largest positive score is added to the selected set and
the process repeats until the budget is used or no shortlisted candidate is
predicted to help.

\subsection{Learning utility and candidate order}

Training uses the observed marginal from \cref{eq:target}. States are sampled
from partially selected context sets so that the same candidate is seen under
different conditions. The response-aware scorer is trained with regression and
pairwise ranking,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L
 =\mathcal L_{\mathrm{Huber}}
 +\beta
 \frac{\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal P}
 |\widetilde m_i-\widetilde m_j|
 \log\!\left(1+e^{-(\widehat m_i-\widehat m_j)}\right)}
 {\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal P}|\widetilde m_i-\widetilde m_j|},
 \label{eq:training-loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal P$ contains candidate pairs from the same state with
$\widetilde m_i>\widetilde m_j$. Regression keeps the score on the scale of
loss reduction. The ranking term places more weight on ordering errors that
forfeit more utility.

\subsection{A theory-shaped response head}

The generic scorer above can learn any interaction between state and response.
\Cref{prop:suff} suggests a more structured alternative. We predict the
state-dependent coefficient and score
\begin{equation}
 \widehat m_B(j\mid A)
 =
 \left\langle g_\theta(h_Q,h_A,h_j),d_{A,j}\right\rangle
 -\|d_{A,j}\|^2-\lambda c_j .
 \label{eq:bilinear-head}
\end{equation}
This head builds the response--residual interaction into the model. For
cross-entropy experiments we also test a learned signed coefficient on the
quadratic response term, allowing the data to adjust the curvature contribution
while preserving the bilinear structure.

\subsection{Stopping and a training-free fallback}

Ranking and stopping use different information. The standard router stops when
the best estimated marginal is non-positive. We also evaluate a forced-budget
variant to separate ranking quality from score calibration.

Pool geometry provides a second decision rule for settings where learning a
candidate ordering is unnecessary or unreliable. Let $\bar\nu$ be the mean
novelty of the candidate pool from \cref{eq:novelty}. With a threshold $\tau$
chosen on held-out targets, the fallback policy is
\begin{equation}
 \pi=
 \begin{cases}
  \text{select by relevance}, & \bar\nu\ge\tau,\\
  \text{use the full pool}, & \bar\nu<\tau.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:adaptive-rule}
\end{equation}
The rule uses no target labels at decision time. Its role is to choose between
two simple deployment behaviours from the information geometry of the pool.

\subsection{Deployment check}

Before a new deployment, we evaluate the router and the full-pool baseline on a
small labelled pilot of targets using the same candidate construction, budget
and loss as the intended deployment. The paired target-level difference gives
the deployment decision. A split-half agreement check can certify a strong
pilot; an unresolved pilot is enlarged. \Cref{sec:experiments} reports the
sample size, temporal transfer and operating characteristics of this procedure.
