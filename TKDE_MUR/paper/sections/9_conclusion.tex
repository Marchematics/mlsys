\section{Conclusion}

Context value changes with the prediction that has already been formed from the
selected set. We capture that change with state-conditioned marginal utility
and show that predictor response is the key observable for estimating it. The
same response--utility structure appears across smooth losses, becomes exact for
Bregman losses, and leads naturally to response-aware and theory-shaped
selectors.

The experiments show when this design pays off and why. Response-aware routing
is strongest when candidate utility is identifiable from the available
response. Stronger predictors can preserve large oracle opportunity while
removing the residual structure needed to rank candidates, and larger candidate
libraries can widen the same gap. Pool geometry explains when relevance helps
or hurts, which supports a simple label-free fallback, while demonstration
selection separates ranking quality from stopping. The practical lesson is
straightforward: choose a context policy together with the predictor and the
candidate pool, because those components jointly determine how much marginal
utility can be seen and realized.
