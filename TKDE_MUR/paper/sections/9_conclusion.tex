\section{Conclusion}

We asked what a router can know about the value of a context before the outcome
is observed. The answer has three parts. Predictor response is the right thing
to observe: for Bregman losses the response--utility relation is an exact
identity, for smooth losses it has a two-sided bound, for softmax cross-entropy
the constants are explicit, and for squared loss the Bayes-optimal score is
affine in the response conditional on the state. What remains to be learned is
the fixed predictor's own residual, so improving the predictor removes exactly
the signal that routing depends on. And the consequence is measurable before
any method is chosen: a cached view that does not correlate with the true
marginal, an ordering that no observable feature recovers, and a label-free
novelty statistic that predicts whether retrieval helps or hurts.

Empirically the three regimes behave as the theory predicts. Where utility is
estimable, learned marginal-utility routing beats fourteen competitors on both
datasets examined --- significantly ahead of the best competitor on three of the
six benchmarks, unresolved on two and below simple pooling on the sixth ---
roughly doubling the strongest non-selection baseline, while
relevance ranking, maximum marginal relevance, determinantal diversity,
k-center, facility location, k-means representatives and random selection are
significantly worse than using no auxiliary context at all. Where it is not
estimable, no method separates from including every candidate --- on one
dataset pooling is significantly better than every alternative, on the other it
ties the response-aware family --- and the oracle's optimal budget is smaller
than the protocol's, so the loss comes from the budget constraint rather than
from bad ranking. Where candidates contribute categorical
evidence, four scoring heads bracket the same ceiling. In each case the
measurement protocol identifies the regime in advance, and in the low-novelty
regime a label-free adaptive rule converts a harmful default into a beneficial
one. Context selection is worth doing exactly when the predictor leaves
something identifiable to select on, and the practical question is therefore not
which ranking heuristic to use but whether the problem in front of us is
identifiable at all.
