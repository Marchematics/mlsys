\title{Response-Aware Context Selection: Marginal Utility, Identifiability, and Adaptive Routing}
\author{Author details to be supplied}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Auxiliary information is useful only through the change it produces in a prediction that already depends on previously selected contexts. We formulate this decision through state-conditioned marginal utility and develop response-aware context selection around it. The theory shows how predictor response determines marginal utility under general smooth losses, becomes exact for Bregman losses, yields explicit control for softmax cross-entropy, and reduces to an affine response model under squared loss. This structure motivates a selector that screens candidates from cached features, probes a small shortlist with the fixed predictor, and reranks the retained candidates with response-aware utility estimates. Across traffic forecasting and demonstration selection, the method is strongest when candidate utility is identifiable from the available response signal. Stronger predictors can absorb the systematic residual that makes candidates distinguishable, leaving substantial oracle opportunity while learned selectors lose the ability to rank it. Pool geometry provides a complementary explanation: candidate novelty determines when relevance helps or hurts, which leads to a label-free adaptive fallback. The resulting framework connects a practical routing method to a general account of when context selection can realize its available value and how a deployment should respond when that value becomes difficult to identify.
\end{abstract}
