\title{Response-Aware Context Selection: Marginal Utility, Identifiability, and Adaptive Routing}
\author{Author details to be supplied}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
The value of auxiliary information changes with the information already
available to a predictor. We study this problem through state-conditioned
marginal utility, the reduction in task loss produced by adding a candidate to
the current context set, and develop a response-aware selection framework around
that quantity. For general smooth losses we derive a response expansion with a
two-sided remainder bound; for Bregman losses the relation is exact; for
softmax cross-entropy the constants are explicit; and for squared loss the
Bayes-optimal utility is affine in the predictor response conditional on the
state. These results motivate a two-stage selector that screens candidates from
cached features and reranks a shortlist with predictor responses, together with
a theory-shaped bilinear head that exposes the response--residual interaction
directly.

The experiments combine a strong positive regime with mechanism tests. With a
linear subset expert on traffic data, response-aware marginal routing
outperforms a broad set of content, fixed-set, utility and learned selectors on
the two datasets examined in detail. Replacing the expert with a stronger
subset-capable nonlinear predictor preserves substantial oracle opportunity but
collapses candidate-level rankability: cached recall falls to chance, observable
probes have near-zero within-episode rank correlation, and no learned selector
separates from pooling. Increasing the candidate library from 16 to 128 raises
the oracle opportunity by about 40\% while driving the router toward random
selection, confirming that more opportunity need not mean more realizable
value. A second task family with CLIP demonstrations separates ranking from
stopping, and a pool-geometry study shows that candidate novelty predicts when
relevance changes from harmful to useful. This mechanism yields a label-free
adaptive rule and a pilot procedure whose deployment verdict is stable on
held-out targets. The resulting picture is constructive: response-aware routing
is effective when marginal utility is identifiable, and the same theory and
measurements indicate when to route, when to pool, and how much of the oracle
opportunity can realistically be recovered.
\end{abstract}
