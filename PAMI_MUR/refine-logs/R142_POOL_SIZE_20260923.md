# R142 — Pool-size scaling: more context does not help

**Date:** 2026-09-23
**Run:** `results/raw/R142_pool_size_20260923_1700` (K ∈ {32,64,128}) plus
`results/raw/R141_adapt_sweep_20260923_1500/ft10` (K = 16)
**Analysis:** `results/derived/R142_pool_size/` via `experiments/analyze_pool_size.py`

## Question

The regime-B result is that response-aware routing does not separate from
pooling every candidate. The obvious objection is that the protocol's pool is
tiny (16 candidates): with a realistic library of hundreds of contexts, pooling
all of them should be infeasible and selection should win. This run tests that
objection directly by holding the budget at four and growing the pool.

Protocol: METR-LA, R101 spatio-temporal transformer adapted 10 epochs, 16
matched cells (targets `0,27,54,82,109,137,164,192` × seeds `101,202`), 4,000
train / 1,000 calibration / 2,000 test episodes, `q ∈ {4,8}`. Only
`--candidate-count` changes between rows.

## Result (16 matched cells at every pool size)

| K | pool all | random four | router `q=4` | router `q=8` | standalone oracle | oracle − router | router − pool |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | +.0057 | +.0005 | +.0076 | +.0087 | +.0851 | +.0775 [+.0675,+.0873] | +.0019 [−.0097,+.0184] |
| 32 | +.0128 | +.0049 | +.0031 | +.0047 | +.0987 | +.0956 [+.0826,+.1088] | **−.0097 [−.0159,−.0022]** |
| 64 | +.0081 | +.0018 | −.0033 | −.0048 | +.1180 | +.1213 [+.1047,+.1378] | **−.0114 [−.0192,−.0018]** |
| 128 | +.0042 | −.0006 | −.0025 | −.0040 | +.1196 | +.1221 [+.1071,+.1363] | −.0067 [−.0180,+.0089] |

## Findings

1. **The objection's premise is true.** Enlarging the pool really does create
   more value: the standalone oracle rises from +.0851 to +.1196, a 40%
   increase, and the paired growth of the identifiability gap is resolved at
   every step --- gap(K=32)−gap(K=16) = +.0181 [+.0126,+.0239],
   gap(K=64)−gap(K=32) = +.0257 [+.0197,+.0315], and +.0446 [+.0376,+.0514]
   over the full range. It saturates between 64 and 128 (+.0008
   [−.0050,+.0061]).

2. **The conclusion is the opposite of the objection.** The router captures
   *less*, not more: from +.0076 (K=16) to −.0025 (K=128). At K=32 and K=64 it
   is significantly *below* pooling (−.0097 [−.0159,−.0022] and −.0114
   [−.0192,−.0018]).

3. **Beyond 16 candidates the router is indistinguishable from random
   selection.** Paired against random four, the router is +.0071
   [−.0045,+.0234] at K=16 and −.0018, −.0052, −.0019 at K=32, 64 and 128 ---
   point estimates at or below zero and never resolved.

4. **A larger shortlist does not rescue it.** `q=8` minus `q=4` is +.0011 at
   K=16 and −.0014, −.0016 at K=64 and K=128, so the response stage is not
   being starved of candidates by the shortlist size. This is worth stating
   carefully: it does not by itself separate "the cached screen is noise" from
   "the marginal is unidentifiable", since a near-random screen of 8 of 128 is
   still near-random. What it does rule out is the simple repair --- buy more
   shortlist slots.

5. **Pooling is flat in pool size** (+.0057, +.0128, +.0081, +.0042, no trend),
   because the expert is trained with subset dropout and tolerates large
   context sets. So the router's decline is not caused by pooling getting
   worse.

## Interpretation

The binding constraint is identifiability, not the supply of candidates.
Enlarging the library raises the value that exists and lowers the share of it
that can be identified, so the practical prescription "retrieve from a bigger
pool" makes the routing problem harder rather than easier. This closes the most
natural escape route from the regime-B negative and turns it into a positive,
testable statement about when context selection is worth attempting.
