# R155 — Does the pilot survive a time shift?

**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Run:** `KBS_MUR/results/raw/R155_pilot_shift_20260925_1000` (192 cells: six datasets × 32 targets × 1 seed)
**Artifact:** `results/derived/R155_pilot_shift/pilot_shift.json`
**Script:** `experiments/analyze_pilot_shift.py`

## Question

R154 validated the measurement protocol as a deployment decision when the pilot
and the deployment are drawn from the same period: a 24-target pilot reproduced
the full-sample verdict 96% of the time. But a pilot is necessarily labelled
*earlier* than the period the router will serve, and that was the one boundary
the paper flagged without measuring. Does the pilot's verdict survive the time
shift?

## Design

The six-dataset ridge protocol was re-run with an added second evaluation split:
after training on the training period, the router and the pool-everything
baseline are scored on the **calibration period** (the pilot) and on the **test
period** (the deployment) for the same targets. Thirty-two targets per dataset,
one seed, 192 targets in total; 7,200 pilot/deployment pairs of episodes.

Supporting change: `--extra-split validation` in
`KBS_MUR/scripts/run_response_router_multitarget.py` and its
`evaluate_seed`, scoring only the two policies the decision needs so the
existing test-split outputs and diagnostic traces are untouched. The KBS suite
(17 tests) passes after the change.

## Result

| dataset | pilot Δ | deployment Δ | pilot verdict | deployment verdict | sign agreement | per-target r |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| METR-LA | +.0079 | +.0042 | AHEAD | AHEAD | .78 | **+.520** |
| PEMS-BAY | +.0203 | +.0354 | AHEAD | AHEAD | .72 | **+.599** |
| PEMS03 | +.0029 | +.0014 | AHEAD | UNRESOLVED | .94 | **+.848** |
| PEMS04 | +.0007 | −.0009 | UNRESOLVED | UNRESOLVED | .81 | **+.648** |
| PEMS07 | +.0041 | +.0048 | AHEAD | AHEAD | .78 | **+.700** |
| PEMS08 | −.0016 | −.0028 | UNRESOLVED | BELOW | .81 | **+.780** |

* **Direction transfers well.** Across 192 targets the within-dataset
  correlation between the pilot's and the deployment's router-minus-pooling
  difference is **`r = +.596` (`p = 8e-20`)**; every dataset is individually
  significant (`+.520` to `+.848`, all `p < .003`). Per-target sign agreement is
  **81%**.
* **Significance transfers less well than direction.** The dataset-level verdict
  — which includes the significance call — agrees on **4 of 6**, against the
  96% same-period agreement of R154. Both disagreements are confidence
  differences with the correct sign, not direction flips: PEMS03's pilot said
  AHEAD on `+.0029` where the deployment gave `+.0014` (unresolved), and PEMS08's
  pilot was unresolved on `−.0016` where the deployment resolved to BELOW.
* **The operationally costly error did not occur.** Four pilots said AHEAD; none
  was contradicted by a BELOW deployment. The two misses were an over-confident
  AHEAD on a genuinely marginal positive (PEMS03) and an under-confident
  non-call on a genuinely negative one (PEMS08) — in both cases pooling or
  routing cost little.
* **Time shift costs roughly what pooling two periods costs, not more.** The
  pilot's estimate is attenuated and noisier than a same-period pilot, so the
  practical recommendation is to enlarge the pilot (R154 measured 96% at `n=24`
  within period; the same `n` across periods gives 4/6 here) rather than to
  abandon the procedure.

## What this changes in the papers

The deployment-procedure section previously carried an unmeasured caveat ("the
pilot measures sampling stability rather than distribution shift"). It now
carries the measurement: **direction transfers (`r=+.60`, 81% sign agreement),
the significance call is right four times in six, and no pilot recommended
routing where routing turned out to be harmful.** That is a weaker claim than
same-period validation and a stronger one than no evidence at all, and it is
stated with both numbers side by side so the reader can see the cost of the
shift.

## Provenance notes

* The run uses one seed per target rather than the three of the six-dataset
  matrix, because a practitioner labels a pilot once. This makes the pilot
  noisier than R154's and is the conservative choice for the claim.
* The pilot and deployment periods are separated by the protocol's own
  train/validation/test split, which is a genuine temporal separation but not a
  change of sensor population or city; the result does not speak to
  cross-domain transfer.
* One seed also means the deployment verdicts here are computed on 32 targets
  with no seed averaging, so they need not equal the three-seed matrix values;
  they are compared against the pilot computed on the same footing, which is the
  comparison the procedure makes.
