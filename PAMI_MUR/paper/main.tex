\title{State-Conditioned Marginal Utility for Multi-Context Prediction}
\author{Author details to be supplied}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Auxiliary contexts do not have fixed predictive value: their contribution
changes as other contexts are selected. We formulate multi-context selection
through set-conditioned marginal utility, the reduction in end-task loss
produced by adding a candidate to the current context set. The formulation
separates structural opportunity from estimation error and applies beyond a
single predictor or task family. We introduce Response-Aware Marginal-Utility
Routing, which narrows the candidate pool with cached representations and
reranks retained candidates using compact predictor-response summaries and a
utility-aware ranking objective. A decision analysis links marginal-utility
estimation error to routing regret, and a general smooth-loss view relates
predictor response to marginal value, with exact and two-sided statements for
Bregman and softmax-cross-entropy losses. The analysis also predicts when the approach pays off:
because the identifiable term is the residual of the fixed predictor itself,
stronger predictors leave less realizable headroom even when the oracle
opportunity persists. Experiments confirm both the gains and the boundary
across standard forecasting benchmarks, a subset-capable nonlinear backbone and
a second multi-context task family, and they separate the ranking, which
transfers, from the stopping rule, which does not. The comparison is not
flattering to the standard toolkit: in the regime where utility is estimable,
learned marginal-utility routing is significantly ahead of the best of fourteen
competitors on three benchmarks, unresolved on two and below simple pooling on
the sixth, while relevance ranking and maximum marginal relevance are
significantly worse than using no auxiliary context at all on four of the six
and the strongest competitor on every benchmark is the trivial policy of
including every candidate; in the regime where it is not, no
method separates from simply using every context, and the sign of the retrieval
heuristic is predicted by a single label-free statistic that also yields a
label-free adaptive rule. Two controlled sweeps locate the mechanism rather
than merely reporting the boundary: adapting the fixed predictor for two epochs
removes about five sixths of the value routing can realize while leaving the
oracle opportunity untouched, and enlarging the candidate library from sixteen
to one hundred and twenty-eight raises that opportunity by forty percent while
driving the router to the level of random selection. Removing the shortlist
entirely changes the result by an amount indistinguishable from zero, which
locates the limit in what the predictor's response can reveal rather than in
the efficiency compromise that observes it. The measurement protocol is then
validated as a deployment decision: on a labelled pilot of twenty-four targets
it reproduces the full-deployment verdict ninety-six percent of the time, with
ninety-eight percent sensitivity for the decision to route at all, and splitting
the pilot in half yields a self-audit that certifies a verdict with
ninety-nine percent agreement.
\end{abstract}
