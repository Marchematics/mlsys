\section{State-Conditioned Context Selection}

Let $S_0$ be an anchor context, $x$ a query, $y$ its target, and
$\candidates=\{C_1,\ldots,C_K\}$ a candidate pool. A selected set is
$A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,K\}$. A predictor produces $f_A(x)$ from $S_0$ and the
contexts indexed by $A$; the predictor is trained before routing and kept
fixed during context selection and router training.

We define the net set utility as
\begin{equation}
 F(A)=\E\left[\loss(f_{\emptyset}(x),y)-\loss(f_A(x),y)\right]
       -\lambda c(A),
\end{equation}
where $c(A)$ is a context cost. The marginal utility of a candidate is
\begin{equation}
 \marginal(j\mid A)=F(A\cup\{j\})-F(A).
 \label{eq:marginal}
\end{equation}
For one observed episode, the training target is the counterfactual loss
difference
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{\marginal}(j\mid A)=\loss(f_A(x),y)-\loss(\fj(x),y)-\lambda c_j.
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
This is a realized observation of the marginal. Its interpretation as an
expectation requires a fixed predictor and a specified state-sampling rule.

For squared loss, let $\mu=\E[y\mid S_0,x,\candidates]$ and let
$\dAj=\fj(x)-f_A(x)$. The marginal separates into
\begin{equation}
 \marginal(j\mid A)=2\dAj(\mu-f_A(x))-\dAj^2-\lambda c_j.
 \label{eq:squared-marginal}
\end{equation}
The first term is the interaction between the current residual and the
direction of the candidate-induced correction, the second term penalizes
overcorrection, and the third accounts for cost. Relevance and standalone
candidate scores do not determine these three terms, and the same candidate
can be useful at one state and harmful at another.

For a smooth loss $\ell(f,y)$ and prediction change
$\dAj=\fj(x)-f_A(x)$, a second-order expansion gives
\begin{equation}
 \ell(f_A,y)-\ell(\fj,y)
 =
 -\nabla_f\ell(f_A,y)^\top \dAj
 -\frac12 \dAj^\top
 \nabla_f^2\ell(\xi,y)\dAj,
 \label{eq:smooth-expansion}
\end{equation}
for some $\xi$ on the segment between $f_A$ and $\fj$. Taking
expectations yields
\begin{equation}
 \marginal(j\mid A)
 =
 -\E\!\left[\nabla_f\ell(f_A,y)^\top \dAj\right]
 +O\!\left(\E\|\dAj\|^2\right)
 -\lambda c_j.
 \label{eq:smooth-marginal}
\end{equation}
When the Hessian is bounded, the second-order remainder is controlled by the
response magnitude. Section~\ref{sec:analysis} turns
\cref{eq:smooth-expansion} into two-sided bounds with explicit constants, gives
an exact identity for Bregman losses that contains
\cref{eq:squared-marginal} as a special case, and shows that response features
are the correct sufficient statistic for the squared-loss case. Predictor
response therefore carries information about marginal utility beyond quadratic
losses.

Two oracle policies expose the structural value of this state dependence. The
standalone oracle ranks candidates by the true standalone marginal
$m(j\mid\emptyset)$ and keeps that order fixed. The sequential oracle
recomputes the true marginal after every addition. Let $G_{\mathrm{stand}}$ and
$G_{\mathrm{seq}}$ denote their prediction gains over the anchor-only
predictor. We measure structural headroom as
\begin{equation}
 H_{\mathrm{state}}
 =
 \frac{G_{\mathrm{seq}}-G_{\mathrm{stand}}}{G_{\mathrm{seq}}}.
 \label{eq:headroom}
\end{equation}
This quantity is an evaluation diagnostic. It isolates the value created by
conditioning on the selected set, independently of router estimation error.
