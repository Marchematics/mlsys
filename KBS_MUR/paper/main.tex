\begin{abstract}
Auxiliary contexts can improve predictive models, yet the value of a context
changes as other contexts are selected. We formulate multi-context selection
through state-conditioned marginal predictive utility, defined by the change in
end-task loss produced by adding a candidate to the current context set. This
formulation exposes decision value that cannot be represented by a fixed
standalone score. We introduce Response-Aware Marginal-Utility Routing
(R-MUR), which first screens candidates from cached representations and then
evaluates predictor responses only for a small retained set. Compact response
summaries are used to estimate marginal utility, while a regression and ranking
objective aligns utility estimation with sequential selection. A decision
analysis connects estimation error to routing regret. Controlled studies show
how redundancy and candidate-set growth change the selection problem, and
evaluations on standard traffic benchmarks show the method significantly ahead
of the strongest of fourteen competitors on three of six benchmarks,
unresolved on two and below simple pooling on one, and ahead of every similarity-, diversity-,
coverage- and utility-based selection policy on all six. The results show
that state-conditioned marginal utility provides a useful decision principle
for budgeted multi-context prediction.
\end{abstract}
