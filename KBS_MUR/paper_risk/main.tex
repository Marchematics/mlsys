\title{Risk-Controlled Context Selection: Deciding When Auxiliary Knowledge Is Safe to Use}
\author{Author details to be supplied}
\begin{abstract}
A system that predicts from auxiliary knowledge must decide not only which
context to use but whether any context is worth using at all. Utility models
are trained to rank candidates, so their scores are not calibrated values: the
absolute error of a utility estimate can exceed the utility it ranks. Acting on
an uncalibrated score therefore selects harmful contexts in a substantial
fraction of decisions. We formulate context selection as a risk-controlled
decision problem with an explicit budget on harmful selections, and we study
the coverage that a router can retain while respecting that budget. The
proposed controller calibrates a selection threshold on deployment states by
conformal risk control, so the expected number of harmful selections per
decision is bounded without distributional assumptions, and it abstains or
stops whenever no candidate can be certified. We show that interval-based
certification, the natural alternative, is far more conservative than the
budget-controlled rule in the regime where utility error dominates utility,
and we characterise when a calibrated threshold remains valid under shift.
Controlled environments with increasing harmful fractions, deployment states
from six standard traffic benchmarks, cross-dataset and cross-run threshold
transfer, and an end-to-end selective routing study support the analysis. The
results turn abstention into a measurable and controllable part of
knowledge-augmented prediction.
\end{abstract}
